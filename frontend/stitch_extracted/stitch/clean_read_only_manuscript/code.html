The rain in New Aethelgard didn't just fall; it dissolved. It was a chemical mist that clung to the neon filaments of the lower wards, turning the world into a smeared watercolor of electric indigo and dying amber. Elias stood at the edge of the cantilevered pier, the haptic sensors in his coat humming as they translated the damp wind into a rhythmic vibration against his spine.

Below him, the sea was a restless abyss of black oil. It swallowed the light of the orbital rings, reflecting nothing back but the void. "The algorithm suggests a change in tempo here," a voice crackled in his inner ear—the dry, synthetic rasp of the Narrative Architect. "Your pulse is spiking. It’s too soon for the confrontation."

Elias ignored the voice. He watched a single freighter drift across the horizon, its silhouette cutting through the smog like a jagged obsidian knife. He could feel the story pulling at him, the inevitable gravity of a plot he hadn't written but was forced to inhabit. The agents were busy in the background, stitching together his past from fragments of data and discarded memories, weaving a noose of causality that he couldn't hope to escape.

"Give me the coordinates," Elias whispered into the cold air. The rain tasted of copper and old circuits. "If the end is coming, I want to see it with my own eyes."

The text continued to flow, emerging word by word on the virtual parchment as the generative engine processed the next sequence of events. There was no cursor, no blinking line—only the steady, relentless march of prose appearing as if from a ghost's typewriter.
